Safety & Handling

Dry ice is extremely cold and releases carbon dioxide gas as it sublimates. Use it only with proper handling and ventilation.

🧤
Use insulated gloves or tongs

Direct skin contact can cause severe frostbite in seconds. Never handle pellets with bare hands.

🚗
Ventilate during transport

Keep fresh air moving in enclosed vehicles and never leave dry ice in an occupied sealed space.

⚠️
Never use an airtight container

Carbon dioxide gas must be able to escape as the dry ice sublimates, or pressure can build dangerously.

🚫
Never touch, taste, or ingest

Keep dry ice away from children and pets, and use it only for its intended purpose.

Dry ice doesn't melt. It sublimates straight from a solid into carbon dioxide gas. In a quality cooler, expect to lose roughly 5–10 pounds per day. Higher temperatures, thin insulation, empty air space and frequent opening all speed that up.

Safety & Handling

Dry ice is about -109°F (-78.5°C) and can cause frostbite-like burns in seconds.

  • Always use insulated gloves or tongs, and never touch it with bare skin.
  • When driving, keep fresh air moving: crack a window and go straight to your destination.
  • Never leave dry ice in a closed car, especially with people or pets inside. The carbon dioxide gas it releases can build up in enclosed spaces.

Store dry ice in a ventilated, insulated cooler in a well-ventilated area, away from children and pets. Don't put it in your home freezer, because it can trip the thermostat or damage the appliance. Never use an airtight container. To dispose of it, let it sublimate in an open, well-ventilated space, ideally outdoors. Never put it in a sink, toilet, drain or trash can.
